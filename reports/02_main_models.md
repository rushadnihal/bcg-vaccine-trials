# Step 2: Fixed-Effect vs Random-Effects Models

_Generated by `R/02_main_models.R` on 2026-11-20 18:45 (R 4.5.3, metafor 5.2.1)_

## Models

- **Fixed-effect**: inverse-variance weighting; assumes one true effect shared by all trials.
- **Random-effects (REML)**: allows the true effect to vary between trials (between-trial variance tau²).
- **Random-effects with Knapp-Hartung adjustment**: uses a t-distribution and adjusted standard errors; gives more conservative intervals when there are few trials.

## Interpretation

Under the random-effects model, vaccination was associated with an estimated **51% lower risk of TB** (RR 0.49, 95% CI 0.34 to 0.70). Heterogeneity was substantial (I² = 92.2%, Q(12) = 152.2, p < 0.0001).

The 95% prediction interval (0.15 to 1.55) is much wider than the confidence interval. It crosses 1, so although the vaccine is protective on average, a new trial setting could plausibly show little or no benefit. This is why explaining the heterogeneity (step 5: meta-regression on latitude) matters.

The fixed-effect estimate (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.60 to 0.70) is closer to 1 because it is dominated by the most precise trials (weight depends on the number of TB events, not just sample size): TPT Madras (1980) alone receives 41.4% of the fixed-effect weight but only 10.2% under random effects. Given the heterogeneity, the random-effects model is the primary analysis.
